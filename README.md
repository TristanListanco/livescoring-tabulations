# Tabulous

Web tabulation for judged competitions. An admin sets up an activity, judges score entries from their own devices, and a public page shows every judge's score and the average, ranked live.

- **Admin panel** (`/admin`): create activities with a name, a score range (min to max), decimal places (whole numbers, or 1, 2 or 3 decimals; a pageant's sub-activities go to 2), whether scores show live (see Live scoring or a deliberation), judges with names and photos, and entries. Every photo (judges, entries and candidates, organizers) opens in a crop step first: drag it to choose what shows, zoom to make it bigger or smaller (or out to fit the whole photo), and it's saved as a small square. Each activity's tabs follow the event day: **Session** (the control desk), **Judges** (the panel, each judge's code and QR code, and approving their devices; the panel can be changed there until the session starts), and **LED wall** for running the show; **Entries** and **Settings** (name, organizer, delete) for setting it up, where test sites add **Test site tools** (reset scores, and deleting the activity even mid-session). The live site doesn't have them, and nor do Vercel previews unless you turn them on (see Deploying to Vercel). Once its session has ended, an activity can be **archived** from the list of activities or its ended Session tab, when no more deliberating is needed: it moves to **Archived activities** (below the list) and from then on can't be reopened, deliberated again or changed, nor taken out of the archive; every judge's device is signed out and their codes stop working, the private tally closes, and the live results stay as they ended without checking for changes. From then on it can only be opened read-only to download its results, score sheets and the judges' notes, or deleted. **Pageant** mode is offered on test sites (locally, in CI and on a testing preview); the live site shows it as coming soon. On the **Session** tab you can hide or show ranks on the public page, and download the official results as a PDF once every judge has scored every entry. The PDF carries a report ID that changes if any score changes, marks each judge's sign-off (Sgd.), or their drawn signature after a deliberation, and prints the organizer's photo and the activity's signatories (for example the Board of Tabulators) with designations, set on its **Settings** tab. Next to it, **Download judges' score sheets** gives a second PDF with one sheet per judge: every entry in running order with that judge's score (and, with criteria scoring, their points for each criterion), signed by that judge and the signatories, under the same report ID. When judges wrote notes, **Download judges' notes** gives them as a third PDF, apart from the results. An archived activity's page keeps the live results link but drops its QR code and the LED wall, whose page is no longer available.
- **Accounts**: the super admin (the developer, using `ADMIN_PASSWORD`) creates, edits and deletes **organizer** accounts with an email, password, organizer name and photo. Organizer passwords need at least 8 characters, a number and a special character; the form ticks the rules off as they're typed. Organizers sign in with their email and see only their own activities; their name and photo show in the admin header, and their photo, as their logo, heads their activities' live results. An organizer can change the password they were given from their **Profile** (their current password first); their other devices are signed out. The super admin sees every activity and can hand one to an organizer from its Settings tab; organizers' activities are listed live ones first, then the newest 50 at a time. Deleting an organizer keeps their activities for the super admin.
- **Judge portal** (`/judge`): a judge enters their six-character code, or scans their QR code (which fills the code in, so they just tap Start judging), to sign that device in. The link itself signs nothing in: a messaging app previewing it, or a QR scanner checking it, would otherwise ask the organizer to approve a device that isn't the judge's. The device shows a pairing code and waits until the organizer approves it on the Judges tab, or from that judge's tile on the Session tab; only one approved device per judge can score, so a stray device that scanned the QR can't. Their screen then waits until the organizer starts the session, then shows the entry the organizer picks. They type the score on a large keypad and submit it, then wait for the next entry. Before a score goes in, the confirmation lays out each criterion's points to check, and after submitting the judge has 5 seconds to undo it; a score confirmed just before the organizer moves on still counts. With live scoring, that's final. In an event without live scoring (and in a pageant), a judge who entered a wrong score can **ask to correct it** while the entry is on screen (or any entry during the deliberation), with a reason: every other judge on their panel gets a pop-up they can't close until they vote (it waits while they're typing or undoing their own score), showing the reason (a pageant's also shows the score and the new one; an event's keeps the scores private), and the score changes only if every one of them agrees (one disagreeing keeps it). A judge alone on their panel asks the organizer instead, who approves or turns it down on the desk. The desk lists every correction and how the vote stands; the results PDFs wait while one is open, then list the corrections that changed a score. Once a score is in, its card lists the points for each criterion, and on a phone the count in the header opens the judge's scores so far (wider screens list them beside the keypad). Judges can keep a note on each entry: under the entry's name on a phone, or in a column beside the keypad from 1280px wide, which can pop out into a window they drag anywhere. Only that judge and the organizer read the notes: the organizer downloads them from the **Session** tab as a PDF of their own, a page per judge under the organizer's logo (the score sheets don't print them).
- **Scoring modes**: simple (one score from a min to a max) or criteria (points for each criterion, adding up to 100, entered on the same keypad). Each criterion can have a lowest score in points, like a score sheet's "lowest possible score" (28 for a criterion worth 40); judges' keypads won't take less. Each can also have a guide for judges (guide questions or what to look for), shown on the judge's screen while they score that criterion; an event's guides can be edited in Settings until judging starts. Criteria totals show on the live results, LED wall and PDF as a percentage or scaled to 10.
- **Judges' names**: a first and a last name, in letters (any alphabet, with the spaces, hyphens, apostrophes and periods names carry, like "Ma. Dela Cruz" or "O'Neil"). Digits and other symbols are refused as they're typed.
- **Pageant mode**: choose **Pageant** when creating an activity, and a step-by-step setup asks for the candidates first (in running order, each with an optional photo for the LED wall and the judges' screens; a list of names pasted into one row fills it and the rows after, running numbers dropped, and Enter goes to the next name), then the judges, then the program. The pageant is created first and each candidate's photo is uploaded after it, one at a time, so a large field never goes over Vercel's 4.5 MB request limit; a draft keeps the photos too, as many as fit. A pageant has two segments, **preliminary** and **pageant proper**, and the admin sets the preliminary's share of the overall score (pageant proper gets the rest). Each segment has its own **sub-activities** (a closed-door interview and national costume; production number, swimsuit, evening wear, Q&A), each with its own scoring, simple or criteria. Shares are typed as percentages of the overall score, the way score sheets write them: with the preliminary at 40%, its sub-activities add up to 40 (an interview at 20, an advocacy at 10, an advocacy video at 10), and pageant proper's up to its first cut add up to the other 60. **Scores start over after a cut**: the sub-activities after it count from zero, their shares add up to 100% (a Q&A alone after the Top 5 is 100%, with no share to type), and they alone rank the next cut, so a cut can only count the sub-activities of its own stage. Adding or taking away a cut refits the shares to the new stages, keeping their proportions, and programs saved before are fitted the same way. Adding a sub-activity when a segment's share is used up splits it evenly to make room, and changing the preliminary's percentage rescales the shares that added up to the old one. A sub-activity can also be scored in **parts**, for one sub-activity with several deliverables (a closed-door interview with its Q&A, the advocacy and the advocacy video): each part has its own scoring, timer and share of the overall score, the sub-activity counts for its parts added up, and judges score each part on its own. The judges are **one board** for the whole pageant or **separate boards** for the preliminary and pageant proper, chosen on the setup's Judges step: each board has its own judges and its own chair (a chair can only move judging in their own board's segment), and each sub-activity waits for, counts, shows and is signed by its own board. Pageant proper needs at least three judges. On the Judges tab a judge other than a chair can still be moved between boards or set to judge both segments. After any pageant proper sub-activity the admin can add a **cut** (Top 10, Top 5): the form shows how many candidates are left before it and flags a size that leaves none out, it ranks the candidates by the sub-activities the admin ticks for it, and only the candidates who go through are judged after it. The last sub-activity ends with the **final cut** (e.g. Top 3), whose order is the final placement. A cut counts each sub-activity as a candidate's average as a percentage of its maximum; when the first cut counts the preliminary with pageant proper sub-activities, the preliminary keeps its share. On the **Session** tab the organizer judges one sub-activity at a time, the whole preliminary before any of pageant proper (the desk won't start pageant proper while a preliminary score is still to come), reviews each cut, breaks any tie on the line (or, in the final cut, a tie for a placement) and confirms it. Candidates keep their number through every cut. A pageant can have **deductions** for violations during the preliminary: turned on at setup (on the Preliminary step, or the Segments tab before the session) with the violations and their points listed, then given to a candidate from the desk's **Deductions** dialog, picking a listed violation or typing another reason and its points, until pageant proper starts. Deductions are points off the candidate's preliminary score, so a cut that counts the preliminary takes them off at its share (2 points, with the preliminary at 40%, take 0.8 off). Only the organizer sees them listed: the preliminary standings and cut PDFs show a Deductions column and each one with its reason, and their report IDs change with them; the live results and the LED wall, which show one sub-activity at a time, never list them. Every sub-activity (and each part, with a standings sheet for a sub-activity in parts), every cut, the final results and the preliminary standings have their own results PDF, and every sub-activity and part has its judges' score sheets too. The program can be changed on the **Segments** tab until the session starts. From then on it's final: the tab is read-only, timers included, even after the session ends. During the show the desk can still give judges more time (**+15 s**, **Restart timer**).
- **Drafts**: an event or a pageant can be saved as a draft at any point while setting it up (**Save draft**), and continued or deleted from the **Drafts** list on the Activities page. Creating the activity clears its draft. Leaving with unsaved changes asks first: reloading or closing the tab gets the browser's prompt, and links, Sign out or switching between event and pageant get a dialog that can save a draft on the way out.
- **Scoring timer** (pageants): a sub-activity can give judges a set number of seconds to score each candidate, for example while they walk the runway. The timer starts when the candidate is shown. Judges' screens and the session desk show it in green while judges can score, yellow when it's about to close, and red once it has, with the time left; after that the server refuses scores until the organizer gives more time or restarts it from the desk.
- **Judging session** (Session tab): before judging, the desk shows which judges have an approved device. The organizer starts and ends judging and shows judges one entry at a time, watching who is still scoring and whether the desk's connection is live. Starting locks the judges and the running order; entries can still be added (until the judges start deliberating, when no one could score a new one) and renamed. Judges can only score the entry on screen while the session is live. Once the session ends, the desk shows the report ID and the results PDF.
- **Live scoring or a deliberation**: an event scores live by default (**Show scores live** when creating it, or in Settings until the session starts): the audience sees each score the moment it's submitted, and a submitted score is final. Without live scoring, the audience sees the entries and a tick for each judge who has scored each one, but no scores (the server doesn't send them) until the judges have signed; the organizer's **private tally** (Session tab, Screens) shows every score so far, judge by judge and criterion by criterion, to tabulate by hand if something fails during judging, with where the show stands as on the live results (judging, the judges deliberating, ended): partial and unofficial until every judge has signed and the session has ended, official from then on, and judges can ask to correct a score. Once every judge has scored every entry, the desk (or the chair, from their own screen) offers **Start deliberation** in place of End session: each judge's screen then lists every entry with their own score, points per criterion, any correction and their notes, to review and correct; then they sign, by drawing their signature on a touchscreen (phone or tablet) or, on a screen without touch, by ticking "(Sgd.)". Signing waits while a correction is open, and a score corrected after anyone signed takes every signature back. The desk shows who has signed, and End session appears once they all have: from then on the results show on the live results page and the LED wall, and the results PDFs (refused until then) print each drawn signature above the judge's name. Reopening an ended session takes the signatures back; once the judges and the organizer have signed, it reopens only by deliberating again, with the password. Pageants always score live for now.
- **Live results** (`/live/<id>`): every judge's score and the average for each entry, updated in real time and made to be projected. Once the session has ended it says **Ended**, with the date and time, in place of Live; during a deliberation it says **Judges deliberating**, and an event without live scoring shows its scores only once the judges have signed. With ranks on, entries are sorted by average and the top 3 stand out; with ranks off, entries stay in running order. A pageant's page follows the sub-activity being judged, with only the candidates still in the running.
- **Privacy policy and terms of service** (`/privacy`, `/terms`): public pages, linked from the start page, both sign-in pages and the foot of the admin panel. The operator, contact address and effective date are in `src/lib/legal.ts`; the privacy policy describes what the app actually does (its cookies, logs, what the public pages show, what deleting removes), so change it together with that code.
- **LED wall** (`/led/<id>`): one entry's scores for the LED wall or video switcher, either as a slim lower third on chroma green (`#00FF00`) to key over the camera shot (one bar along the bottom with the entry, a tile per judge and the average, so the camera keeps the rest of the frame), or as a full-screen scoresheet. A pageant's wall shows the sub-activity being judged, with its name under the candidate's, and the candidate's photo from the setup beside it in both displays. Scores fade in as judges submit them, or, with **Show scores only when every judge has scored**, all at once when the last judge is in. With **Keep judges anonymous**, each judge's name and photo on the wall becomes a "?". The wall's **Look** is chosen there too: a font (standard, elegant serif, modern, condensed or classic capitals), a colour theme (navy, black and gold, crimson, emerald, royal purple, midnight teal, rose gold, sunset amber, berry, graphite, or a light pearl, champagne or sky, each keeping its text readable) and square, soft or rounded corners, for the green screen and the full screen alike. The green screen itself stays chroma green. The wall follows the entry being judged, fading each entry and score in; the admin picks the display in the **LED wall** tab.

Built with Next.js 16, Supabase (Postgres, Realtime and Storage) and Tailwind CSS 4.

## Setup

1. **Create a Supabase project** at [supabase.com](https://supabase.com).
2. **Create the database.** In the Supabase dashboard open **SQL Editor**, paste the contents of [`supabase/schema.sql`](supabase/schema.sql) and run it. It creates the tables, the score rules, the change feed live screens listen to over realtime, and the photo storage buckets. Running it again is safe.

   Databases set up before a feature existed need the migrations in [`supabase/migrations/`](supabase/migrations/). Once the release pipeline is set up (see [Releasing](#releasing)), new ones reach both databases from GitHub when they're merged; to bring a database up to date by hand, run them in order: `003_led_wall.sql` (also covers 002) for show ranks and the LED wall, then `004_organizer_accounts.sql` for organizer accounts and the LED display settings, then `005_judging_session.sql` for judging sessions, then `006_devices_criteria_signatories.sql` for judge device approval, criteria scoring and PDF signatories, then `007_entry_photos_result_decimals.sql` for entry photos and result decimals, then `008_judge_names_and_chair.sql` for judges' first and last names and the chair, then `009_led_transition.sql` (optional now: it added an LED wall animation choice that has since been removed), then `010_led_anonymous_judges.sql` for keeping judges anonymous on the LED wall, then `011_pageant_mode.sql` for pageant mode (segments, sub-activities, cuts and the scoring timer), then `012_pageant_parts_and_drafts.sql` for sub-activities scored in parts and saved drafts, then `013_judge_panels.sql` for separate boards of judges, each with its own chair (if you ran an earlier copy of 013, run it again: it's safe to repeat), then `014_led_look.sql` for the LED wall's font, colour theme and corners, then `015_deductions_and_themes.sql` for pageant deductions and the LED wall's newer colour themes, then `016_private_results.sql`, which makes scores and the other results tables private (see How it works below), then `017_sign_in_limits.sql` for limits on guessing admin passwords and judge codes (until it's run, sign-ins work without the limit), then `018_judge_notes.sql` for judges' notes (until it's run, judges' screens leave notes out), then `019_score_corrections.sql` for score corrections (until it's run, judges aren't offered them and scores stay final), then `020_session_end_and_archive.sql` for when a session ended and archiving (until it's run, the live results say "Ended" without the time and activities can't be archived), then `021_deliberation.sql` for live scoring on or off, the deliberation, judges' signatures and three decimal places, and two indexes for many activities at once (until it's run, every event scores live), then `022_admin_sessions.sql` for admin sessions that can be ended (until it's run, sessions work as before; once it is, admins sign in again once), then `023_indexes.sql`, three indexes that keep deleting an entry or an activity, and the super admin's list of organizers' activities, quick however many scores and activities there are (best run between shows), then `024_session_restart_rule.sql`, the rule that a session with scores can't go back to not started, for databases that ran 021 before it was added there (it changes nothing on one that has it), then `025_judge_corrects_own_score.sql`, for a judge correcting their own score in an event without live scoring, then `026_tie_breaks_and_organizer_signoff.sql` for breaking ties in the deliberation and the organizer's signature that ends it, then `027_activity_signatories.sql` for signatories set on each activity, which starts each one with its organizer's (until it's run, every activity prints its organizer's), then `028_score_window.sql`, so the database itself refuses a score once judging has ended, and a score a judge confirmed just before the organizer moved on still counts (until it's run, such a score is refused and the app alone checks the session), then `029_atomic_transitions.sql`, which makes the database finish what used to take the app several writes (deliberating again or reopening clears every signature in the same change; archiving signs every judge's device out in it) and keep rules the app followed on its own (the deliberation starts only with every score in, no entry is added during it, and a judge has one approved device at most). Until it's run, the app makes these changes itself, as before. Each only adds tables and columns, so a deployed older version keeps working after you run it, except 016: an older version's screens then stop hearing realtime changes and check every few seconds instead, until this version is deployed.
3. **Configure the app.** Copy `.env.example` to `.env.local` and fill it in. The Supabase values are under **Project Settings > API Keys**. Keep real values out of `.env.example`, because that file is committed.
4. **Run it.**

   ```bash
   npm install
   npm run dev
   ```

   Open http://localhost:3000/admin/login?as=super and sign in with `ADMIN_PASSWORD` as the super admin. Create organizer accounts on the **Organizers** page.

### A live database and a testing database

Use two Supabase projects, each set up with `schema.sql`: one for the live site, and one for testing (running locally, the web tests and Vercel previews). Rehearsals, test runs and previews then never touch a real event.

- `.env.local` has the testing project's values. `npm run dev` and `npm run test:e2e` use it.
- `.env.production.local` has the live project's values. `npm run build` and `npm start` use it (Next.js reads it ahead of `.env.local` in production mode), so a laptop running the event at the venue scores into the live database. Give it every variable: one missing there falls back to `.env.local`.
- The two use different `SESSION_SECRET`s, so a sign-in from testing never works on the live site.
- Run each new migration on the testing project first, then on the live one.
- Don't use `vercel env pull` to fill `.env.local`: Vercel's Development environment has none of these values (sensitive ones can't go there), so it would replace your testing settings with a file that has none of them.

## Running an event

1. Create the activity in the admin panel. You land on the **Judges** tab.
2. Give each judge their code, or have them scan their QR code. Judges can also open `/judge` and type the code. Each device shows a pairing code: approve the matching one on the **Judges** tab, or from the judge's tile on the **Session** tab.
3. Open the public results link on the venue screen, or share it with the audience.
4. In the **Session** tab, start the session, then show each entry in turn. When every judge has scored, show the next one. End the session after the last entry.
5. For the LED wall, open the LED wall link full screen on the computer that feeds the wall or video switcher, and key out the green. The wall shows the entry being judged on the **Session** tab; choose the display in the **LED wall** tab.
6. Use **Reset scores** after a rehearsal to start clean, from **Settings > Test site tools**. It also deletes the judges' notes, signs every judge's device out (judges sign in again with their codes, and you approve the new devices) and puts the session back to not started. Neither is on the live (production) site, nor in any other production build (a Vercel preview, or `npm start` on a laptop) unless `TEST_TOOLS=on` says it uses a testing database.
7. After the last entry, end the session. The Session tab then shows the report ID and **Download results PDF**.

### Many activities at once (breakout sessions)

Fourteen activities live at once with three judges each (42 judges' devices, a desk and an LED wall per room) were load tested on the testing project: all their screens stayed connected and scores kept arriving, with room to spare, at many times the pace of a real show. Two things decide whether a long day holds up:

- **The database's size.** Supabase's smaller compute sizes run on burstable CPU: they take short bursts of load well, but after a long stretch of heavy load they slow right down (in testing, saving a score went from a fraction of a second to many seconds). For an event day with many activities, use at least the Pro plan and a compute size above the smallest, and watch CPU on the project's dashboard; compute can be raised for the day and lowered after.
- **The audience's phones.** They check for changes rather than holding realtime connections, so a crowd costs a small read every few seconds each (and each read is a line in Supabase's logs, which its plans now meter). Prefer projecting the live results in each room over hundreds of phones following them.

### Running on a laptop over the venue Wi-Fi

Judges' phones and tablets must be on the same Wi-Fi as the laptop. For the event itself, use a production build; it is faster and more reliable than the dev server, and it uses the live database from `.env.production.local` (see A live database and a testing database):

```bash
npm run build
npm start -- -H 0.0.0.0
```

The admin panel works at `http://localhost:3000/admin` on the laptop. Judge links, QR codes and the results link automatically use the laptop's Wi-Fi address (for example `http://192.168.1.16:3000`) so other devices can open them. Set `NEXT_PUBLIC_SITE_URL` to pin a specific address.

`npm run dev -- -H 0.0.0.0` also works for testing on phones; the dev server allows this laptop's network addresses.

#### With nginx in front, for a real event

On its own the app can't tell the devices on the Wi-Fi apart. Next.js doesn't give server actions the connection's address, so the limits on guessing judge codes and admin passwords count by the `X-Real-IP` and `X-Forwarded-For` headers, which any phone can send. [`deploy/nginx/tabulous.conf`](deploy/nginx/tabulous.conf) puts nginx in front: it replaces those headers with each device's real address, lets go of stalled connections, refuses oversized requests, and caps sign-in attempts per device (judge sign-in and admin sign-in only; scoring is never slowed). The app then listens on the laptop alone, so every device goes through nginx.

1. Install nginx: `brew install nginx` on a Mac, `sudo apt install nginx` on Ubuntu or Debian.
2. Include the config inside nginx's `http` block. On a Mac: `ln -s "$PWD/deploy/nginx/tabulous.conf" "$(brew --prefix)/etc/nginx/servers/tabulous.conf"`. On Linux: `sudo cp deploy/nginx/tabulous.conf /etc/nginx/conf.d/`, and remove the package's welcome site (`sudo rm /etc/nginx/sites-enabled/default`), which would otherwise answer on port 80 instead. Check it with `nginx -t`.
3. Start the app on the laptop only, then nginx:

   ```bash
   npm run build
   npm start -- -H 127.0.0.1
   brew services start nginx   # Linux: sudo systemctl reload nginx
   ```

The admin panel is then at `http://localhost/admin`, and judge links, QR codes and the results link use the laptop's Wi-Fi address without a port (`http://192.168.1.16`). nginx listens on port 80: if it can't open it (another app has it, or the system wants it started with `sudo nginx`), change both `listen` lines in the config to another port, such as 8080, and open that port instead. It doesn't add HTTPS: phones only trust a certificate for a real domain, so on venue Wi-Fi sign-ins still travel unencrypted. For that, run the event on Vercel.

## Deploying to Vercel

Import the repository in Vercel and add the same environment variables. Set `NEXT_PUBLIC_SITE_URL` to your production URL.

The server functions run in Seoul (`icn1`, set in [`vercel.json`](vercel.json)), the region of both Supabase projects: every page and action makes a few trips to the database in a row, and from Vercel's default region (Washington, D.C.) each one crosses the Pacific. If you move the database, move the functions with it.

Give the live project's values to the **Production** environment only, and the testing project's to **Preview** (pull requests and branches), with their own `SESSION_SECRET` and `TEST_TOOLS=on`. Previews hide the test site tools, which reset scores and delete activities, unless `TEST_TOOLS=on`: a preview given the live variables by mistake can't wipe a real event. Running locally (and in CI) always has them.

## Releasing

Pull requests deploy to Vercel previews on the testing database; `main` deploys to production. Three things keep a release safe; each needs a one-time setting.

**Only tested code reaches production.** In GitHub, protect `main` (Settings > Rules > Rulesets, or Branches): require a pull request and the CI checks **Lint, types and unit tests**, **Schema and migrations** and **Web tests**. In Vercel, add the same checks as Deployment Checks (Project > Settings > Deployment Checks), so a production deployment goes live only once they've passed.

**Migrations reach the databases from GitHub** (`.github/workflows/migrate.yml`): merged into `main`, a new file in `supabase/migrations/` is applied to the testing project, then to the live one once you approve it. Each project records what it has run, so nothing runs twice by accident. To set it up:

1. Create a Supabase access token (supabase.com > Account > Access Tokens) and add it to the repository's Actions secrets as `SUPABASE_ACCESS_TOKEN`.
2. Create two GitHub environments (Settings > Environments), `testing` and `live`. In each, add the variable `SUPABASE_PROJECT_REF` (the project's id, from its URL) and the secret `SUPABASE_DB_PASSWORD` (Project Settings > Database). On `live`, add yourself as a required reviewer: live migrations then wait for your approval.
3. Tell each project which migrations it has already run (it only records them once the pipeline runs them). For the live project, list the ones you've run there; as of 18 October it had run 002 to 023: `npx supabase migration repair --status applied 002 003 004 005 006 007 008 009 010 011 012 013 014 015 016 017 018 019 020 021 022 023 --linked --project-ref <live ref>`. The testing project is already recorded through 023. The pipeline runs everything after that (024 first), the next time a migration is merged or when you run the workflow by hand.

Writing a migration: the next number (`024_what_it_does.sql`), and the same change in `supabase/schema.sql`, which creates new databases. A migration only adds: never drop or rename anything the deployed version still uses, since the database is shared by the version before and the version after, and a rollback goes back to the version before. Remove it in a later migration, once no deployed version uses it. Make it safe to run twice (`if not exists`, `create or replace`). Never change a migration once it's merged: a database that has run it won't run it again, so the change would never reach it (the session rule added to 021 after the live project had run it is why 024 exists). Put the change in a new one. `npm run check:migrations` checks all of this against `main`, and CI runs it on every pull request.

**Production changes between shows.** A deploy reloads every screen that's open (a judge loses what they were typing), so don't let one go live mid-show. Either don't merge while a show is running, or turn off automatic promotion (Vercel > Settings > Environments > Production > Auto-assign Custom Production Domains): each production build then waits until you promote it. Skew Protection (Vercel Pro) lets screens open before a deploy carry on with the version they loaded. To undo a release, Instant Rollback switches back to the previous deployment; the migrations only add, so the version before keeps working on the database as it is.

Node.js is pinned in `package.json` (`engines`), which Vercel and CI both use.

## How it works

- All writes go through Next.js Server Actions using the Supabase secret key. The browser only gets the publishable key, which can read nothing but `activity_changes`: one row per activity saying when it last changed, kept up to date by database triggers. Scores, judges, entries, activities and programs are private, so hidden ranks and anonymous judges can't be read around the app.
- Judge access codes live in a separate table with no public access.
- Database triggers check every score against the activity's range and decimal places, and reject any change to a submitted score except through an approved correction (see Judge portal above), which the database applies in the same transaction that settles it. A score that changes after judges signed takes every signature back, only an event without live scoring can deliberate, and its session can't end from the deliberation until every judge has signed (migration 021).
- Sessions are signed, http-only cookies (`SESSION_SECRET`). Admin sessions last 12 hours and judge sessions 3 days. To change the secret without signing everyone out (mid-event, say), set `SESSION_SECRET_PREVIOUS` to the old value and `SESSION_SECRET` to the new one, and redeploy: sessions signed with either keep working, and every new one is signed with the new secret. Remove `SESSION_SECRET_PREVIOUS` after 3 days, once the last session it signed has run out. Changing `SESSION_SECRET` alone signs everyone out at once, which is what you want if it leaked. Organizer passwords are hashed with scrypt; changing one signs that organizer out on other devices, and deleting an account ends its sessions. Changing `ADMIN_PASSWORD` signs the super admin out everywhere.
- Each admin sign-in is also a row in `admin_sessions`, named by its cookie, and checked on every request (alongside the account, so it adds no wait). Signing out deletes the row, so the session ends wherever its cookie is, a copy included; an admin's profile lists the browsers signed in to their account and signs any of them out. Every app instance checks the same table and the same signature, so any instance can serve any request.
- Guessing is limited. Within 15 minutes, 10 wrong admin passwords from one network address, 50 for one account, or 25 wrong judge codes from one address (judges at a venue often share one) refuse further tries from it until the oldest fall out of the window. Failures are kept in a server-only table and cleared after a day. On Vercel the address comes from Vercel's own headers; a server started with `next start` trusts the browser's, so there only the per-account limit holds.
- A judge link opened on a device already approved for another judge asks before switching, since switching signs it out and needs the organizer's approval again.
- Photos are checked by their contents, not the type the browser declares: only JPEG, PNG and WebP are stored, with the type their bytes show.
- Every response tells browsers not to let other sites frame the app or guess file types, sends other sites only the origin as the referrer (judge links carry codes), and turns off the camera, microphone, location and payment features the app never uses.
- Every page has a Content Security Policy with a nonce of its own (`src/proxy.ts`): only the page's own scripts run, so a script slipped into a page wouldn't, and a page can talk only to this site and the Supabase project, so nothing could be sent elsewhere.
- The private tally (an event's scores before the judges sign) is the only page that carries those scores: the desk and the LED wall tab are sent which judge has scored which entry, corrections without the scores, and the deliberation's ties and signed results worked out on the server. It opens only with the signed-in admin's own password, entered on the tally itself, every time: for that browser and that admin session alone, for 10 minutes, after which it locks itself. The unlock is a signed cookie sent only to the tally's address and never to another site, so a copied link opens nothing; wrong passwords count against the sign-in limits, and every opening and refusal is logged (`tally_opened`, `tally_refused`) with who and on what device. A browser can't stop a screenshot, so the tally is made hard to leak instead: it carries a watermark with the viewer's name and the time, can't be selected, copied or printed, and blurs while the page is out of view (as in a phone's app switcher).
- The public pages (live results and LED wall) get only what they show: judges' first names with a last initial and their photos (on an LED wall that keeps judges anonymous, not even that), and no scores until they're released (only which judge has scored which entry). Of the organizer, only their logo and name (its description) appear, on the live results; a pageant's list of violations stays off them.
- Server actions can be called with anything, not just what the screens send, so each checks its arguments: a session can only start, deliberate or end (never go back to not started, which the database refuses too while it has scores), and votes and choices must be true or false.
- The test site tools (reset scores, delete mid-session) are off in every production build unless `TEST_TOOLS=on`, including `npm start` on a laptop at the venue, which uses the live database.
- Running `npm start` on a laptop, there's no platform in front of the app to vouch for where a request came from: the per-address sign-in limits trust the browser's own headers there unless nginx is in front with `deploy/nginx/tabulous.conf` (see Running on a laptop over the venue Wi-Fi), which replaces them with each device's address. Everyone on the venue Wi-Fi shares the limits on Vercel (one public address). Sign in to the admin panel before the show; sessions last 12 hours. A judge code on its own is no use to a stranger: their device still needs the organizer's approval.
- Known and accepted: the public key can read `activity_changes`, so anyone can see activities' ids and when they last changed (never what), which the screens' live updates need. The lint tooling depends on a `braces` with a denial-of-service advisory and no fix yet; it only ever reads this project's own files.
- Organizer accounts and password hashes sit in a table with no public access. Every admin action checks that the signed-in organizer owns the activity it touches.
- Live pages check every few seconds whether their activity changed (one row of `activity_changes`, read straight from Supabase, so a check costs no page render) and reload their data from the server only when it did; judges', desks' and LED walls' pages also hear the change over Supabase Realtime, which makes it immediate, and while it's connected they check only every 20 seconds (the LED wall every 6), as a safety net. A missed notice or a dropped connection costs seconds, not a stale screen. Realtime's plan counts connections at their peak, so a page out of view for a minute lets go of its connection (and connects again as it comes back), every screen lets go of it once judging has ended (checking every half minute instead, the LED wall every 10 seconds), and an archived activity's pages don't check at all. The audience's live results don't hold a realtime connection (with many activities at once their phones would be most of realtime's connections, and it falls behind when each change has to reach hundreds), check every 4 seconds while on screen (not while the phone is locked), stretching to every 12 seconds while nothing changes (between entries) and back at the first change, and read through a short shared server cache keyed by when their activity last changed, so a page asked for after a change is never served the board from before it, whatever order the change and the cache's clearing happen in. Every page also refreshes when it comes back into view.
- The busiest paths keep trips to the database few, since every one is paid on every screen of every activity running at once: a judge's screen is two rounds of reads, submitting a score one read before the write, and showing the next entry from the desk one before the change.
- Screens ride out a dropped connection or a failing server and come back by themselves; none is ever left on an error page until someone reloads it. A screen only reloads its data once a check has just reached the server, and checks less often the longer they fail (with a little randomness, so a room of screens doesn't retry in step). A refresh that can't get through, or that the server fails, is asked for again (at once when the device is back online) while the screen keeps what it last showed, with a note (not on the LED wall); actions aren't sent again automatically, since one that reached the server before the connection dropped would happen twice, except a judge's score, which the server recognises; and a judge's screen, the live results and the LED wall that fail to load say "Reconnecting…" and try again by themselves. An action is given up after 25 seconds, and the server gives up on a call to the database after 10 (30 for a photo upload); the database stops a query after 8. A judge's score that didn't get through is sent again twice before the judge is asked: the server knows a score sent twice.
- Work that would hold up other requests happens off their path. Results PDFs are drawn on a worker thread, one per server, so a big one (a whole activity's judges' notes takes a second or two) doesn't make the judges' requests to that server wait; each PDF's `Server-Timing` header says where it was drawn. Photos are cropped and shrunk in the browser before they're sent, and a new pageant's candidates' photos go up ten to a request. Clearing up (deleting a removed photo or a deleted activity's photos, expired sessions, old sign-in failures) happens once the response has been sent.
- Rankings use the average of the scores submitted so far, rounded to two decimals. Entries with equal averages share a rank.

## Observability

The server writes one JSON object per line to its log (on Vercel: the project's **Logs**), so lines can be searched by field, counted, and turned into percentiles. Nothing secret goes in: no passwords, judge codes, cookies or query strings.

| `event` | When | Fields worth watching |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `action` | Every server action and export, once answered | `action`, `outcome` (`ok`, `refused` with the person's `error`, or `failed` when it threw), `ms` in all, `dbMs` of it waiting on the database over `dbCalls` calls (`dbErrors` failed), `request` (Vercel's request id) |
| `db_error` | A database or Storage call that failed (not a duplicate a retry expects) | `path` (the table or file), `status`, the database's `code` and `error` |
| `slow_db` | A single database call over 500 ms | `path`, `ms` |
| `request_error` | A page, route or action that threw | `route`, `kind`, `digest` (the reference the admin's error screen shows) |
| `client_problem` | A screen reporting what went wrong on it | `kind` (`score_unreachable`, `action_unreachable`, `out_of_date`, `realtime_down`, `screen_error`), `where`, `device` |
| `runtime` | Each minute, per server, in production | `loopP99Ms` (how late its timers run: a server too busy to answer promptly), `rssMb`, `heapMb` |
| `score_not_saved` and others | A write that failed, named for what it was | `code`, `error` |

- **Latency:** p95 and p99 of `ms` per `action` (not the average: a few slow saves are what judges notice), with `dbMs` saying how much of it was the database.
- **Errors:** `failed` actions, `db_error`s and `client_problem`s per action or screen. A `refused` action is the app saying no (a validation message), unless `dbErrors` is above 0.
- **Saturation:** `runtime.loopP99Ms` and memory per server; database CPU and connections in the Supabase dashboard; Realtime's own share of database time in the show report below.
- Vercel keeps runtime logs only for a limited time, shorter on the free plan. To keep a show's worth and chart percentiles, add a log drain to a log service.
- **The database over one show:** [`supabase/show-report.sql`](supabase/show-report.sql) resets the database's query statistics before a show and lists afterwards where its time went: each query's calls, mean, spread and worst case.

## Tests and CI

GitHub Actions runs on every pull request and every push to `main` (`.github/workflows/ci.yml`):

- **Lint, types and unit tests**: `npm run lint`, `npm run typecheck`, `npm test`.
- **Schema and migrations**: `npm run check:migrations`: a database set up from `main`'s `schema.sql` and brought up to date by this branch's new migrations matches one created from this branch's `schema.sql`, and each new migration is safe to run twice.
- **Web tests**: starts a throwaway local Supabase on the runner, loads `supabase/schema.sql`, builds the app and runs the Playwright tests in `e2e/` against it. They walk through a whole event: creating an activity with a judge photo, judges scoring on a phone and a tablet, the live board updating in real time, hiding ranks, the results PDF and the judges' score sheets, the LED wall, and resetting and deleting from Settings on a test site. A second run takes a pageant from its step-by-step setup (candidates reordered and given a photo that shows on the LED wall in both displays, separate boards of judges for the preliminary and pageant proper, and deductions for violations listed) to the final results: a cut with a tie on the line that the organizer breaks, a scoring timer that closes and is reopened, and each sub-activity's, cut's and segment's results PDF. They also check sign-in guards and scan key pages for WCAG 2.1 AA issues. The HTML report is attached to each run.

To run the web tests locally, start the app with `npm run dev` and run `E2E_PORT=3000 npm run test:e2e`. They use your `.env.local` project, create an activity named `E2E …` and delete it at the end.
