## Platform

web

## Users

- **Organizers (primary):** event and production companies that run pageants and judged competitions for their clients. They set up an activity, run the judging session from a laptop at the control desk while the show is live, and hand the client signed official results. They do not sign up themselves: the super admin creates each organizer account.
- **Judges:** the invited panel. They are often not technical and have never seen the product before. Each one scores from their own phone or tablet, in a single sitting, while the show runs. One judge is the chair of the board of judges and can move to the previous or next entry from their own screen.
- **Audience:** people at the venue, or watching a broadcast, who follow the scores on the projected live results page, on their own phones, or on the LED wall and broadcast lower third.
- **Board of Tabulators and signatories:** the people who sign the results PDF. Each activity has its own, set on its Settings tab, and their names and designations print as signature lines.
- **Super admin:** the developer. They create, edit and delete organizer accounts, can see every activity, and can hand an activity to an organizer.

## Product Purpose

Tabulous is the tabulation for a judged event. Judges score each entry from their own devices. Averages and rankings update the moment a score comes in, on the live results page and the LED wall. When judging ends, the organizer downloads an official results PDF that cannot be quietly changed. Success means a whole event runs with no paper scoresheets and no spreadsheet tally. Judges and organizers get through it without training, the audience sees the scores as they happen, and nobody can dispute the final results.

## Positioning

At most events today, judges fill in paper sheets and tabulators add them up in Excel. Tabulous replaces both with one system, where every score is:

- **Final and traceable:** with live scoring a submitted score is final. Without it, a judge can correct their own score, saying why, until they sign their scores after deliberating (in a pageant, a correction needs every other judge on the panel to agree, or for a judge alone on their panel the organizer's approval); the database rejects any other change, and the results PDF lists every correction with its reason. Only an approved device per judge can score. The PDF's report ID changes if any score changes, and each judge's sign-off is recorded: (Sgd.), or their drawn signature.
- **On screen instantly:** the same score reaches the live results page, the LED wall and the broadcast lower third the moment the judge submits it.
- **Usable cold:** judges and organizers who have never used it can run an event with no training.

The selling point is all three together, integrity and broadcast-speed output, in a tool non-technical people can use under show pressure.

## Operating Context

- **Before the show:** the organizer creates the activity: name, score range and decimal places (up to 3), whether scores show live, simple or criteria scoring (criteria add up to 100, each optionally with a lowest score in points), the judges with photos (each cropped and resized when it's chosen), the chair, and the entries in running order. A pageant is set up step by step instead: the candidates first, each with an optional photo for the LED wall and the judges' screens, then the judges, then the program (see Pageant mode). Resetting scores after a rehearsal, and deleting an activity mid-session, are test site tools, in Settings. They are hidden and refused on the production Vercel site, so organizers there can't reset an activity. Resetting also deletes the judges' notes and signs every judge's device out, so judges sign in again for the next run. Pageant mode is offered on test sites only for now: the production Vercel site shows it as coming soon.
- **Device pairing:** each judge enters a six-character code, or scans a QR code that fills it in for them to tap Start judging. Their device shows a pairing code, which the organizer approves on the Judges tab.
- **During the show:** the organizer runs the Session tab as the control desk. They start the session, which locks the judges and the running order, show one entry at a time, watch who has scored, and end the session (ending asks for the organizer's password). Judges see only the entry on screen and type their score on a large keypad, with each criterion's guide (if the organizer wrote one) while they score it. The confirmation lays out each criterion's points before a score goes in, and a submitted score can be undone for 5 seconds. Judges can keep a note on each entry, which only they and the organizer read. An event without live scoring keeps the scores from the audience: a judge can correct their own, saying why, until they sign, and once every entry is scored the organizer or the chair starts the deliberation, where each judge reviews their scores and notes and signs (drawn on a touchscreen, or "(Sgd.)" ticked without one). Every tie in the ranking is broken first: it shows at the top of every judge's screen, and the chair puts the tied entries in order and says why (scores stay as given; the results PDF lists each tie and how it was broken), and no one signs until it is. Once every judge has signed, the results come up over the organizer's desk and stay there until the organizer signs them too (drawn, or "(Sgd.)") with their password, which ends the session; the results go out then. An ended deliberation can be deliberated again, with the organizer's password: everyone signs again, and judges whose devices were signed out sign in again with their code.
- **Venue screen:** the live results page (`/live/<id>`) is projected or shared with the audience. With ranks on, entries are sorted and the top 3 stand out once every judge has scored them; an average still waiting on judges shows quieter. With ranks off, entries stay in running order. The entry being judged is marked Now judging. On a projector or other wide landscape screen the page fits exactly one screen: rows size to it, and a list too long for one screen pages through by itself (turning to a page when its scores come in), with how the board works in a footer. Phones and portrait screens scroll. Before the session the page says judging hasn't started; once it has ended, it says Ended, with the date and time. An event without live scoring shows no scores there until the judges have signed.
- **LED wall and broadcast:** the LED page (`/led/<id>`) runs full screen on the computer that feeds the wall or video switcher. It shows either a lower third on chroma green `#00FF00`, keyed over the camera shot, or a full-screen scoresheet, each with the entry's or candidate's photo beside the name when it has one. The wall follows the entry being judged. The organizer picks the display, the animation, whether scores wait for every judge, whether judges stay anonymous (a "?" on the wall, Judge 1, Judge 2 and so on on the live results page, with no names sent to either), and the wall's look (font, colour theme and corners) from the LED wall tab. Nothing but the graphic is on air: moving the mouse or pressing a key at the wall's computer shows, for a few seconds, whether it's connected and what it's showing, with a Full screen button. An event without live scoring has no LED wall: its scores stay with the judges until they've signed, so there's nothing to show.
- **Drafts:** setting up an event or a pageant can be saved as a draft and continued later from the Activities page. Leaving with unsaved changes asks first.
- **Pageant mode:** a pageant has a preliminary segment and a pageant proper segment, each with a share of the overall score set by the organizer. Each segment has sub-activities with their own simple or criteria scoring and a share of the overall score, as score sheets write it (a 40% preliminary's sub-activities add up to 40); a sub-activity with several deliverables can be scored in parts, each with its own scoring and share, adding up to the sub-activity's. Cuts after pageant proper sub-activities (Top 10, Top 5) narrow the field, ranked by the sub-activities the organizer ticks for each one. Scores start over after a cut: the sub-activities after it add up to 100% (a lone Q&A is 100%) and only they rank the next cut; the last sub-activity ends with the final cut, whose order is the final placement. The judges are one board for the whole pageant, or separate boards for the preliminary and pageant proper, each with its own chair: with separate boards, only the preliminary's judges judge the preliminary and only pageant proper's judge pageant proper. Pageant proper needs at least three judges. Once the session starts the program is final and the Segments tab is read-only, even after it ends. A pageant can have deductions for violations during the preliminary: the violations and their points are listed at setup, and the organizer gives them from the desk (a listed one, or another with its reason typed) until pageant proper starts. They're points off the preliminary score, counted at the preliminary's share in a cut that counts it, and only the organizer sees them listed: on the desk and in the standings PDFs, with their reasons. The organizer judges one sub-activity at a time from the Session tab, the whole preliminary before pageant proper starts, breaks ties on a cut's line (and for a final placement) by choosing, and confirms each cut. A sub-activity can have a scoring timer: green while judges can score, yellow as it closes, red once it has, when the server refuses scores until the organizer gives more time. Every sub-activity, cut, the final results and the preliminary standings have their own PDF.
- **After the show:** once every judge has scored every entry, the organizer downloads the results PDF. It carries the report ID, the judges' sign-offs, the organizer's photo, and the activity's signatories' signature lines. A second PDF has each judge's score sheet: their scores (and points per criterion) in running order, signed by that judge and the signatories, under the same report ID. Pageants have one for each sub-activity and part. The judges' notes download as a PDF of their own, apart from the results: a page for each judge, every entry with their note on it, under the organizer's logo. An activity whose session has ended can then be archived, which is how the organizer says no more deliberating is needed (the ended desk offers it too): it leaves the list of activities for the archived ones and can't be reopened, deliberated again or changed after that, only read (its PDFs still download) and deleted. Judging closes for good: every judge's device is signed out and their codes stop working, the private tally closes, the live results stay as they ended without checking for changes, and its LED wall is no longer available.
- **Environment:** a live event venue, with judges' phones and tablets on the venue Wi-Fi. The app runs either as a production build on the organizer's laptop or as a Vercel deployment. Either way it needs to reach the Supabase project. An organizer can run many activities at once (breakout sessions: 14 rooms of three judges each), and the Activities page keeps itself current with what each running room needs from them (devices to approve, the next entry to show, a session to end), those rooms first; the server runs next to the database (Seoul) so every screen stays quick, and the database's compute size decides how long a heavy day holds up.

## Capabilities and Constraints

- **Built on:** Next.js 16 (App Router), Supabase (Postgres, Realtime, Storage) and Tailwind CSS 4. All writes go through Server Actions. The browser holds only the publishable key, which reads nothing but when each activity last changed: results stay private.
- **Accounts:** organizers see only their own activities. Organizer passwords need at least 8 characters, a number and a special character. Judges' first and last names are letters (with the spaces, hyphens, apostrophes and periods names carry). Organizer accounts and judge access codes sit in tables with no public access.
- **Live updates:** live pages check every few seconds whether their activity changed and reload from the server only when it did; judges', desks' and LED walls' pages also hear it over Realtime at once (and then check only now and then, as a safety net), while the audience's pages only check, less often between entries, so a crowd of phones doesn't crowd out realtime. Pages refresh when they come back into view. To keep within the database plan's realtime connections and logs, a page out of view for a minute lets go of Realtime, every screen does once judging has ended (checking every half minute), and an archived activity's pages stop checking.
- **Rankings:** rankings use the average of the scores submitted so far, rounded to two decimals. Equal averages share a rank.
- **Criteria totals:** shown as a percentage or scaled to 10 on the live results, LED wall and PDF.
- **Fixed requirement:** the LED lower third's chroma green is there so the switcher can key it out. It is not a styling choice. The lower third is one slim bar along the bottom, so the camera keeps most of the frame.
- **Admin themes:** the admin panel has light and dark themes. The judge screens, the live results page and the LED wall keep their own fixed appearance.
- **Terms:** activity, event, pageant, entry (a pageant's candidate), running order, segment (preliminary, pageant proper), sub-activity, part, share, cut, draft, final cut, deduction (for a violation), scoring timer, live scoring, deliberation, signature, judge, chair (of the board of judges), organizer, super admin, session (not started, live, ended), archived, access code, pairing code, judge device, simple and criteria scoring, live results, LED wall, lower third, scoresheet, signatories, Board of Tabulators, report ID, Sgd.
- **Undecided or unrecorded:** the business model, pricing, and how event companies find and join Tabulous. Future work must not invent them.

## Brand Commitments

- **Name:** Tabulous (tabulation, but fabulous), used throughout the product. It was called LiveScoring before; the repository and its folder keep that name.
- **Voice in the shipped copy:** plain, short and direct, written for people under time pressure. Examples: "I'm a judge", "Show it below when the contestant is ready."

## Evidence on Hand

- **Real-world use:** none yet. Tabulous has only been used in rehearsals and has not scored a real event. Nothing may claim real events, clients, testimonials, usage numbers or reliability records.
- **Test suite:** the Playwright tests in `e2e/` walk through a whole event. They cover creating an activity with a judge photo, judges scoring on a phone and a tablet, the live board updating in real time, hiding ranks, the results PDF and the judges' score sheets, the LED wall, and resetting and deleting. A pageant run covers the step-by-step setup, cuts with an organizer-broken tie, the scoring timer, and the pageant's results PDFs. They also check sign-in guards and scan key pages for WCAG 2.1 AA issues.
- **Logo:** a tally gate, four strokes and the slash that counts a fifth, in one colour (`src/components/brand.tsx`: the mark, and the wordmark beside the name). The app icon (`src/app/icon.svg`, `favicon.ico`, `apple-icon.png`) is the mark in mint on a navy square. The PDFs carry it small in the footer; the live results and the LED wall carry only the organizer's logo.

## Product Principles

1. **Integrity first.** A submitted score changes only through a recorded correction with its reason, and every published number traces back to a judge's approved device. No convenience feature may weaken that.
2. **No training needed.** A first-time judge holding their own phone, or an organizer at the control desk mid-show, should always see the next action without being told.
3. **The organizer runs the show.** The organizer, and the chair from their own screen, decide what is on. Judges see only the entry in front of them, and the audience sees only what has been submitted.
4. **Every output is public.** The live results page, the LED wall and the PDF are seen by an audience, keyed into a broadcast, or signed as the official record. They have to hold up projected, on air and in print.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

The target is WCAG 2.1 AA. Axe runs in the Playwright suite to check it. Judges score on small phone screens under venue lighting, often without having used the product before, so the controls they rely on must stay large, high-contrast and easy to understand.
